## Version 4: Long bio (280 words)

For: Personal website, full Amazon Author Central page, press kit, book club guide

> Ashish Tyagi is a founder, software developer, and longtime reader. His debut novel, *Ethos: The Promise*, is a literary corporate thriller about a young CEO facing the hardest eighteen hours of his life — the night before a hostile takeover that will decide whether the company he built to honor his dying sister's memory survives, or is sold for parts.
>
> Ashish holds master's degrees in mathematics, computer science, and business — a combination that has shaped both his career and his curiosity. He has spent much of that career building products and companies at the intersection of those three disciplines, and writing was the natural place those questions ended up: when the spreadsheet asks you to compromise everything you love, what do you do? When the easier path is in plain view, what holds you steady?
>
> He is interested in the conversations that happen in the white space of corporate life — the late-night drives, the unfinished cups of coffee, the moments when a mentor says one sentence that changes everything. *Ethos: The Promise* is his attempt to put one of those conversations on the page.
>
> Outside of building and writing, he reads widely and cares deeply about the people inside the systems we build. He believes that the integrity of a company eventually shows up in the lives of the people it touches — for better or for worse.
>
> Ashish lives in the United States. *Ethos: The Promise* is his first novel. He is at work on a second.
>

## Author photo guidance

Most platforms ask for an author headshot. Specs that work everywhere:

- **Size:** 800×800 px minimum (square)
- **Format:** JPG or PNG
- **Style:** Clean background, soft natural light, looking at camera. Indie-author convention is "approachable, slightly serious" — neutral expression, not stiff, not posed.
- **Avoid:** Group photos, sunglasses, baseball caps, busy backgrounds, ultra-low resolution
- **Budget option:** Have a friend take it on a recent iPhone with good light against a plain wall — totally fine for KDP/Apple/Google.
