## 1. CANONICAL FACTS (the bible)

## 2. CONTINUITY CONTRADICTIONS (exhaustive)

### A. Aaron's age when Priya died — six conflicting versions
- **Ch1 line 33:** "Aaron had been sixteen. He stood at the cremation…"
- **Ch1 line 141:** "thirteen-year-old Aaron swearing to a universe that had just stolen his sister…"
- **Ch5 line 117:** "He'd been twenty-one years old, a junior in college studying computer science" (this is the day Priya asked the physics question, "morning of the day she got sick").
- **Ch6 line 55:** "Aaron had been eighteen years old. Priya had been eleven. He'd been home from Berkeley for the summer."
- **Ch9 line 105:** "I was seventeen years old and had to watch my sister die."
- All five must collapse to **16**.

### B. Priya's cause of death — two incompatible diseases
- **Ch1:** persistent cough → asthma + lung infection that a predictive algorithm could have flagged.
- **Ch5 line 99:** "She had a bacterial infection that should have been easily treatable. But our pediatrician didn't have access to the same genomic data that a specialist three states away would have had. So he prescribed a drug that her system couldn't metabolize properly. It built up in her kidneys."
- These are not paraphrases of one event. Pick one and rewrite the other.

### C. The college: MIT vs Berkeley
- Aaron is consistently described as MIT (ch1 line 71, ch2 line 107, ch3 line 141, ch5 line 223 calls Sarah "college roommate at MIT," ch12 line 45 says "their final year at MIT").
- Ch5 line 91: "the fire escape of their Queens apartment" — fine, this is high school.
- **Ch6 line 55:** "He'd been home from Berkeley for the summer." Strike "Berkeley" → "home from MIT."

### D. The hostile-takeover vote — book votes on it FOUR DIFFERENT WAYS
- **Ch9 line 149:** "The motion passed unanimously. Seven votes to zero. Not a single board member voting with Damian." (Vote at 9:15 AM.)
- **Ch13 line 213:** "The vote was 8-0 in favor of the API pivot, with one abstention and one opposed." (Reconsideration vote, same body.)

- **Ch15 line 7:** "The vote tally remained on the projection screen like a monument: 7-6. One vote." Twelve-member board now? Eight-member board in ch13?

Recommend: 7–6 with Dalton as the swing (the ch14/ch15 version makes the swing-vote drama work). Then ch9's "unanimous" must be cut entirely — it pre-resolves the plot eight chapters early.

## 3. PACING & ENGAGEMENT (chapter by chapter)

**Ch1 (Vishada).** Strong, vivid opening ("The glass walls of the 50th-floor boardroom…"). Middle is a long Damian-backstory infodump (lines 43–57) that stalls momentum just as the despondency is being established. Aaron's "Is the kingdom worth the slaughter?" lands; Chris's entrance is well-staged. Closes with strong forward momentum. Aaron's monologue-of-thought between lines 43–97 runs ~1,200 words without dialogue or external action.

**Ch2 (Sankhya).** Opens with atmospheric rain-on-windshield (good). Middle slogs into a Chris infodump about the Dutch East India Company and the Roman Empire (lines 73–81) — ~600 words of monologue that pulls the reader out of the car and into a history lecture. Closing has good "Take me back to the office" momentum. Risk: every Chris speech longer than 300 words is pure exposition (flagged often).

**Ch3 (Karma).** Decent opening, "The silence in the executive suite of Ethos Tech was absolute. It was 2:00 AM." Middle drags through three long file-by-file infodumps (Sarah's vulnerability, Marcus's short position) presented as inert paragraphs rather than scenes. Chris's "balanced pen" set piece works visually. Closes well.

**Ch4 (Jnana Karma Sanyasa).** Strong setting (war room). The Sattva/Rajas/Tamas teaching takes over the middle and stays there for 70% of the chapter; this is the first place the Gita allegory becomes too on-the-nose ("'This is the Sattva phase'… 'This is the Tamas phase'"). The Damian-arrival-dinner flashback (lines 71–95) is one of the strongest action beats in the book. Close ("clarity… and now, at last, he understood it") is too neat for a chapter that hasn't moved any external stakes.

**Ch5 (Karma Sanyasa).** Opens well (espresso ritual). MAJOR slog: a 2,000+ word reverie about Jennifer in Big Sur (lines 39–61) that bears no relation to the imminent vote and stalls all forward momentum. Sarah-in-Napa flashback also adds little. Chapter ends with Aaron walking *toward* the meditation room — should END inside the meditation room, not on the threshold.

**Ch6 (Dhyana).** Solid premise (meditation chapter) but the chapter is essentially one long internal monologue. Priya's "let yourself fall" memory is the strongest beat. The "I am not my thoughts" moment is on-the-nose but works in context. Chapter closes with overlong philosophical wrap-up — could cut last 30 lines.

**Ch7 (Jnana Vijnana).** Opening is fine. Middle is one of the worst monologue zones in the book: Chris delivers ~3,500 words of "lower nature vs higher nature" philosophy inside a descending elevator. The floor-number ticking device tries to give it structure but is mechanical. NO external stakes for the entire descent.

**Ch8 (Akshara Brahma).** Aaron knocks and enters! Best chapter for plot momentum in Part 2. Strong "I have a structural anomaly" reveal of the EU investigation. Closes on Aaron yielding the floor and walking out — proper cliffhanger.

**Ch9 (Raja Vidya).** Plot resolves prematurely. Vote passes 7–0. This kills all suspense for the remaining 9 chapters and contradicts ch10–14 directly. Apart from the structural problem, the chapter has the best business-strategy content in the book — Aaron's API pivot pitch is concrete and credible.

**Ch10 (Vibhuti).** Pacing breakdown: opens at "2:32 PM" after the 9:15 AM vote in ch9. Then Chris monologues about Elena's mother for ~1,500 words. Elena's backstory is moving but it's a third-hand info-dump. The Aaron speech at the end ("you asked me earlier if I could guarantee these numbers") is strong.

**Ch11 (Vishvarupa).** The "Universal Form" visualization set piece is the most cinematic moment in the book. Works. Risk: the descriptions of red nodes/blue nodes consuming/healing get repetitive and over-explained for ~3,000 words. Cut by 30%.

**Ch12 (Bhakti).** Strong opening. Middle features the duplicate Elena paragraphs (see §2.M). Aaron's confrontation of Sarah's father story works on first read, then is repeated by Chris in ch10 and again in narration — three telling of the same trauma is too many.

**Ch13 (Kshetra Kshetrajna).** Procedural-warfare opening is fine. Aaron's "physical collapse in the bathroom" passage (lines 57–77) is the most human moment in the back half of the book. Chris's "Field/Knower" teaching is the cleanest didactic chapter — keep. The chapter ends with another vote (8–0), redundant with ch9.

**Ch14 (Gunatraya).** Auditor scene is well-staged with slow tension. But this is the THIRD vote and contradicts the first two. The chapter is ~70% interior monologue describing the Gunas (Sattva/Rajas/Tamas) as Aaron observes each board member. By the time of the announcement, the reader has heard the same triad explained four times across the book.

**Ch15 (Purushottama).** Quiet chapter, mostly Aaron reflecting + Chris's farewell. Vote tally shifts AGAIN to 7–6. The Chris farewell is moving ("You've never not seen me… You've only ever failed to recognize me"). Then Aaron's solo reflection runs ~3,500 words to the chapter's end with very little event. Cut by 40%.

**Ch16 (Daivasura).** Decent time-jump opening. The "Daivi vs Asuri" framing risks becoming a sermon (and does). Aaron's HR decisions are described in the abstract, not dramatized — could be a scene where he fires Richard, isn't. Headlines list (lines 53–65) is the most "show, don't tell" moment.

**Ch17 (Shraddhatraya).** Six-months-later jump works. The Shraddha (faith) lecture on the whiteboard (lines 41–67) is ~1,000 words of pure didacticism — should be condensed into a tighter scene with Marcus. The European-consortium / Volkmann setup is concrete and grounded.

**Ch18 (Moksha).** Strong premise (Friday 5 PM deadline). The opening pages establish stakes well. But the climactic email and visualization-comes-alive sequence runs into a series of denouement monologues. Chapter ends with ~80 lines of meditative reflection AFTER the victory — same problem as ch15. Cut the last 1,500 words; let the blue light spreading speak for itself.

## 6. THEMATIC/STRUCTURAL ENGAGEMENT NOTES (top 5 things that would make a reader put the book down)

1. **The vote happens three or four times.** Ch9 ends with the 7–0 unanimous vote against Damian. Ch13 ends with an 8–0 reconsideration vote. Ch14 has the auditor announce a one-vote-margin outcome. Ch15 has the screen show 7–6. A reader paying attention will reach ch10 confused (we just won? why is everyone still in the room?) and reach ch15 actively annoyed. **This is the single biggest plot problem.** The cleanest fix: cut ch9's vote entirely. Make ch9 an extended pitch with no vote; reconsideration in ch12; final vote in ch14 at 7–6; ch15 reflects on it.

2. **The Gita allegory goes on-the-nose by ch4 and never recovers.** Each chapter title names its Yoga. Each chapter has a character (usually Chris) explicitly naming the Sanskrit concept being illustrated ("This is Sattva," "This is Vibhuti," "This is the Knower"). The brief asks whether the allegory becomes too on-the-nose — yes, by ch4. By ch14 every character is being explicitly labeled as Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic. Suggestion: keep the chapter titles but pull the explicit naming out of the dialogue; trust the reader to recognize the resonance.

3. **Sarah is barely a character.** The brief asks whether Sarah ever speaks in person — yes, but minimally:
   - Ch1: voicemail only.
   - Ch6: "How long?" in the hallway.
   - Ch8: barely speaks; reads.
   - Ch9: "What about the data protection issues?"
   - Ch10: described in tears; doesn't speak.
   - Ch12: silent, then crying.
   - Ch15: "We have a lot of code to rewrite."
   - Ch16: described leading a team; no dialogue.
   - Ch17: described crying; no dialogue.
   - Ch18: described laughing; minor "I just realized I'll be telling my children…"
   Sarah is the co-founder, the MIT roommate, the betrayer, the redemption. She has approximately 30 lines of dialogue across 100,000 words. The reader has no idea who she is as a person beyond her grief-story and her competence. **Give her at least one scene where she drives action.**

4. **The climax is anticlimactic because the boardroom victory is announced multiple times before ch18.** By ch14 the vote is won. By ch15 Chris has departed. By ch16 we're in the aftermath. The "real" climax (ch18, the European deal) is a separate climax that the reader has not been pacing toward. The book has two endings stacked on top of each other and neither lands as decisive.

5. **Aaron's transformation is told, not enacted.** From ch4 onward Aaron is increasingly described as detached, transformed, liberated, free. But the only chapter where he *acts* under pressure with stakes on screen is ch8/ch11 (the EU investigation reveal and the visualization). Most of his other appearances are interior monologue or him being walked through Chris's teaching. The brief notes the risk of Gita-chapters drifting into philosophical monologue. The risk is realized in ~10 of the 18 chapters.
